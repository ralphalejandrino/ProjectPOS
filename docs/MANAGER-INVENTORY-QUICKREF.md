# Manager Quick Reference — Inventory & Profit

One page. Keep it by the POS.

**The whole idea:** you tell the POS **three things**. It does all the math — costs, stock, profit — for you.

---

## 1. Record what you BUY  (every delivery)

**Ingredients → find the item → Restock.**

- Tap **"Enter by the package instead"**, choose the package (sack, box, bottle), and say how many base units are in one package **once** (e.g. 1 sack = 25,000 g). It remembers this next time.
- Enter **how many packages** you bought and the **price per package**. Done.

The POS converts it to grams/ml for you, updates the stock, and updates the ingredient's cost as a **running average** (old stock + new delivery). **You never divide a sack into grams.**

---

## 2. Keep RECIPES right  (set once, update when a drink changes)

Each drink lists its ingredients and how much it uses per serving.

- Get the amounts right once. From then on, the POS knows **exactly what each drink costs to make** and the **profit** on it.
- When you change a recipe (new supplier size, new ingredient), update it here — the cost updates itself everywhere.

---

## 3. COUNT at the week's close  (a few minutes, once a week)

**Ingredients → Weekly Count.**

- The POS lists only the items **worth counting** (things running low, plus counted items like tea bags).
- Type **what's physically on the shelf**. The POS shows the difference from what it expected and records it as **waste**. That's your shrinkage report.

Do this at open or close — never during the rush.

---

## What the POS does for you (so you and your staff don't)

- **Deducts ingredients on every sale** automatically — cashiers just ring the order.
- **Tracks each ingredient's cost** as prices change (running average).
- **Shows "Can make now"** for each drink instead of a fake per-drink stock number.
- **Works out COGS, profit, and margin** for the week.
- **Flags what to reorder** before you run out.

---

## Reading your weekly numbers  (Reports → Weekly)

| Number | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Gross Profit** | What you keep after the cost of ingredients sold. This is your real earnings. |
| **Gross Margin %** | Profit as a share of sales. Higher is better. |
| **Profit by Item** | Which drinks actually **make money** — not just which sell the most. |
| **Ingredients to Reorder** | Buy these before the weekend. |
| **Net Cash Flow** | Cash in − what you spent restocking this week. This is **cash movement, not profit** — a big delivery week can look negative and still be fine. |

---

## Two kinds of ingredients

- **Metered** — the POS counts it down on every sale: milk, beans, syrup. (Most things.)
- **Counted** — you count it by hand: tea bags, garnishes. The POS still adds its cost to the drink, it just doesn't guess the amount per sale.

Set this on each ingredient under **Type** when you add it.

---

## Rules of thumb

- Record deliveries the **day they arrive** — that keeps costs and profit accurate.
- If a drink's **profit looks wrong**, check its recipe amounts first.
- Prices drift — that's fine. The running average handles it; you don't adjust cost by hand.
- Counting is a **weekly** habit, not a daily one.
